Methods and design notes (v0.3.0)

This page has the derivations, design choices and simulation results behind the changes summarised in the README. Benchmark scripts are in bench/, and the release notes are in NEWS.md.

Stage 1: interventional total effects

estimate_total_effects regresses every readout on [intercept, knockout indicator, donor dummies] once per knockout, then forms the Wald ratio Θ[u, j] = Δ[j] / Δ[u]. The published estimator took the univariate OLS slope of readout j on readout u over KO(u) ∪ controls, which mixes the interventional contrast with the within-control covariance of u and j — the confounded association the perturbation was meant to bypass — and applied no donor adjustment even though the model carried donor terms. The estimator also returns delta-method standard errors (95% coverage 0.95 in simulation) and, optionally, the full within-knockout covariance.

Weak knockdowns

The gain from the new stage-1 estimator depends on knockdown strength. A Wald ratio divides by the knockdown effect Δ[u], so when a knockdown barely moves its own gene (first-stage F < 10, a weak knockdown) that row's ratios become heavy-tailed. Over 100 simulated 8-gene datasets per design cell, the new estimator had lower total-effect RMSE than the pooled slope in 66–88% of datasets, and its median RMSE was 27–58% lower. Its mean RMSE, however, was only modestly lower and in one cell higher (0.357 vs 0.326), because weak knockdowns (6–13% of rows) give it a heavy tail. Restricted to rows with F ≥ 10, it won in 96–100% of datasets, with mean RMSE 43–63% lower. estimate_total_effects warns about weak knockdowns, and weak_knockdowns(te) lists them. A knockdown can be weak because the guide is inefficient, or because the gene's control expression is already near zero, so removing it changes little. Stage 2 weights each row by its standard error, so weak rows have little influence there.

Stage 2: the exact total-effect identity

The noisy estimates are no longer used as a design matrix. For a hard intervention the normalised total-effect matrix is

T̃ = Diagonal(diag(A))⁻¹ A,        A = (I − B)⁻¹

Row u of A(I − B) = eᵤᵀ gives A[u,v] = Σ_w A[u,w] B[w,v] for v ≠ u; dividing by A[u,u] is exactly the parent recursion the published get_cyclic_matrices encoded. Note this is not (I − B)⁻¹ unless the graph is acyclic: a hard knockout cuts the edges into the perturbed gene, so loops through it must be removed, which is what the row normalisation does. Writing the moment condition this way puts the model on the left and the data only on the right, which removes the errors-in-variables attenuation of the original t = T β formulation, and the free per-block σ is replaced by the stage-1 sampling variances.

Dispersion φ

A dispersion φ multiplying those variances is available (estimate_dispersion = true) but is fixed at 1 by default: the system is just-identified, so no residual degrees of freedom inform φ, and sampling it creates a funnel. On a 5-gene test, fixing it took rhat from 2.18 to 1.006.

Deterministic log density and analytic gradient

The published spectral_radius seeded power iteration with Base.rand inside the model, so repeated evaluations at the same parameters differed slightly, which adds noise to L-BFGS and NUTS; it also computed the spectral radius of the elementwise square W ∘ W. The stability guard is now a deterministic, differentiable bound on the largest singular value, and the non-differentiable sum(abs.(W); dims = 1) penalty is replaced by a choice of smooth shrinkage priors (:studentt by default, :horseshoe, :normal). The gradient of the whole posterior is written in closed form — differentiating the matrix inverse gives Aᵀ G Aᵀ — so no automatic differentiation is involved and the cost is one factorisation plus two matrix products regardless of how many coefficients are sampled.

Sampling: NUTS with a Pathfinder metric

The published analysis used Pathfinder's Gaussian approximation as the posterior. Here Pathfinder supplies the warm start and, crucially, its diagonal-plus-low-rank covariance is used as the HMC metric: the moment likelihood is strongly anisotropic, and with a diagonal metric step-size adaptation collapses toward zero (in testing, step size ~1e-4, rhat ≈ 3, ESS ≈ 5). With the Pathfinder metric the same problem samples at rhat ≤ 1.01 with ESS in the hundreds.

Memory: no dense T matrix

get_cyclic_matrices allocated zeros(nv*(nv-1), nv*(nv-1)) before sparsifying, so the allocation grew as nv⁴: 784 MB at 100 genes, 4.0 GB at 150, 12.7 GB at 200, 64 GB at 300. The replacement likelihood stores only the p × p total-effect matrix — 1.4 MB of model state at 300 genes, with one log-density-plus-gradient evaluation costing about 25 ms there with one BLAS thread when the stability penalty is active (34 ms with eight; Julia 1.13).

The closed-form estimator

When every readout is also perturbed, the identity inverts exactly: B = I − T̃⁻¹ Diagonal(diag(T̃⁻¹))⁻¹. This costs one p × p inversion (12 ms at 300 genes, 0.3 s at 2,000 on Julia 1.13) and is the flat-prior MAP. fit_llcb uses it as the optimiser's starting point. fit_llcb_closed_form(te) resamples total effects through it to give uncertainty without MCMC. It is unweighted, so a single weak knockdown can distort it badly. On 10-gene simulations its RMSE was 0.082 with no weak knockdowns, but 1.1–5.3 with two. Use it for a quick first look or for very large p, and check weak_knockdowns(te) first.

Threading

Chains run on Julia threads, and BLAS is pinned to one thread while they sample (blas_threads = :auto) to avoid oversubscription. Its previous setting is restored afterwards.

Identifiability

With p perturbed genes read out on the same p genes there are exactly p(p−1) moment equations and p(p−1) coefficients, so stage 2 is just-identified: all posterior uncertainty comes from stage-1 noise propagated through the identity, and all sparsity comes from the prior. Reading out more genes than you perturb is supported (Θ may be q × p with q < p) but makes the system under-determined, so the prior carries more weight.

The ported published model

Version 0.3.0 also ports that stage 2 to current Turing. The port uses the same settings as the published analysis:

  1. Each Pathfinder run is initialised from Uniform(−0.03, 0.03). Pass init = :published to start instead from the moment-based point computed in fit_cyclic_model.
  2. The optimizer is LBFGS(m = 8).

Expect statistically equivalent but not identical numbers: the random streams and Pathfinder internals have changed since then. The discrete-DAG sampler (fit_model) was not part of the published analysis and has not been ported.

Convergence in simulation

On six simulated 10-gene datasets (bench/stability.jl, Julia 1.13.1), fit_llcb reached rhat < 1.05 on all six (max 1.015). The minimum ESS was 308, 0.01% of transitions were divergent, 95% coverage was 0.963, and mean AP was 0.886 against a base rate of 0.222.
